How Silicone Roof Coatings Improve Energy Efficiency

Back to Blog

On a July afternoon in Texas, the roof over your head is hotter than boiling water. A dark commercial membrane routinely hits 150–160°F, and your air conditioning spends all day pulling that heat back out of the building. Paint it white with silicone and the same roof runs up to 50°F cooler — which shows up on the power bill every month for the next twenty years.

The Science Behind Cool Roofing

Everything here follows from one number: how much sunlight your roof sends back. A dark membrane absorbs most of what lands on it, conducts that heat through the insulation, and hands it to your HVAC as extra load. A white silicone surface reflects up to 85% of it straight back to the sky before it ever becomes heat.

This reflectivity is measured by two key metrics:

  • Solar Reflectance Index (SRI): Silicone coatings typically achieve an SRI of 100 or higher, compared to 5-20 for traditional dark roofs.
  • Thermal Emittance: The ability to release absorbed heat. Silicone coatings excel here, preventing heat buildup.

Real-World Energy Savings

Cool roof studies consistently show peak cooling demand falling 10–15% and annual air conditioning use falling 20–35%. On a 50,000 sq ft commercial building in Houston, that is typically $15,000–$25,000 a year. Note what that means over a 20-year warranty term: the energy saving alone can exceed what the coating cost to install.

Additional Benefits Beyond Energy Savings

While energy efficiency is a major advantage, silicone roof coatings provide several other benefits:

  • Extended Roof Life: By reducing thermal shock (the expansion and contraction from temperature changes), silicone coatings can extend your roof's lifespan by 15-20 years.
  • Waterproofing: Silicone is naturally waterproof and won't chalk, crack, or peel over time.
  • UV Protection: The reflective properties also protect the underlying roof membrane from UV degradation.
  • Reduced Urban Heat Island Effect: Cool roofs help lower ambient temperatures in urban areas.

Is Silicone Coating Right for Your Building?

Silicone works best on the flat and low-slope roofs that cover most commercial buildings, and it bonds over nearly every substrate you are likely to have. See how it compares with acrylic and aluminum if you are weighing systems. It can be applied over:

  • Modified bitumen
  • Built-up roofing (BUR)
  • Single-ply membranes (TPO, EPDM, PVC)
  • Metal roofs
  • Spray polyurethane foam (SPF)

Tax Benefits and Incentives

The IRS generally treats roof restoration as a maintenance expense, which means it can be deducted in the year the work is done rather than depreciated over 39 years. Many utilities also offer cool roof rebates, and the work may contribute to LEED credits. Confirm the tax position with your CPA — it depends on the scope of work — and check the rebate before you start, since some require pre-approval.
