Why Dallas-Fort Worth Businesses Choose Silicone Roof Pros

No Texas metro is harder on a flat roof than DFW. 105°F in August, hard freezes in January, 40-degree swings inside a single day, and hail often enough that the region has a nickname for it. Every one of those cycles works the seams a little looser.

Silicone handles that because it stays flexible. It expands and contracts with the deck instead of cracking, so freeze-thaw cycling does not open it up. The reflective white surface also drops roof temperature sharply through the North Texas summer, taking load off your HVAC.

From Downtown Dallas to the Stockyards, Las Colinas to Arlington, we work roofs across the metroplex. And on a hail-damaged roof we will tell you straight whether coating is the right call or whether you should be filing an insurance claim for a replacement instead.

Dallas-Fort Worth's Flat Roof Challenges

Our silicone coating system is engineered to handle North Texas's toughest conditions

Extreme Heat

A dark DFW roof can reach 160°F in August. Coated white, it runs up to 50°F cooler — which slows the ageing of everything underneath and cuts what your chillers have to pull out of the building.

Hail Damage

Hail rarely punctures a membrane outright. It bruises it, and those bruises open into leaks two summers later. A silicone layer absorbs much of that impact, and where hail does mark it, we recoat the affected area rather than replacing the roof.

Temperature Swings

A 40-degree swing between noon and midnight moves every panel, lap and fastener on your roof. Rigid coatings crack under that cycling. Silicone flexes with it, season after season.

Severe Storms

Forty-plus thunderstorm days a year, most with wind behind the rain. Wind-driven water gets under laps and flashings that ordinary rain never reaches — and a seamless membrane has no laps to get under.
